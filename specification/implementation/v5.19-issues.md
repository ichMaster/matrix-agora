# v5.19 — Issues

Issues for phase **v5.19 — Analytics: the spending by agent and by service, over a chosen period** (version **v5 —
Voice**), derived from the Tasks and DoD in [ROADMAP.md](../ROADMAP.md) §v5.19 and the contracts in
[ARCHITECTURE.md](../ARCHITECTURE.md). This file covers one phase; ids continue from the previous phase (AGORA-166 →
**AGORA-167…170**).

The phase adds an **Analytics** view to the panel. The owner picks a period and an agent (or all of them) and sees
the spending: by service (`engine`) and its models, by kind, by day and, for all agents, by agent. Each service shows
its calls, tokens, characters, audio and video seconds and its cost.

The view has **charts and trends** (the owner, 2026-11-01: «мають бути графіки і тренди»):
- the cost a day by service with its moving average;
- the running total against the previous period's;
- the calls a day, and the shares;
- the sparklines and changes from the previous period;
- the weekly slope, and the month at this rate.

It also shows the subscriptions' prorated fee and has a CSV download. The CLI report gets the same analysis.

It reads the usage lines as they are (usage line v6). No line or field changes. The daily report and the dashboard's
7-day Tokens card stay as they are.

## v5.19 — Analytics: the spending by agent and by service, over a chosen period

### AGORA-167 — The analysis: `analyze()` by service, model, kind, day and agent, the trends, the previous period and the subscriptions

**Description:**
A pure function in `agents/usage.py` turns the parsed usage lines into the analytics report for one period and one
agent (or all of them). The panel's route and the CLI both call it. It reuses `Row`, `cost`, `parse_model_prices`
and `parse_second_prices`. A parser for `PRICE_SUBSCRIPTIONS` is new.

**What needs to be done:**
- **`Row` gains `audio_ms`** (Deepgram's `audio_ms`), added in `add` and in `aggregate`. Nothing else in the existing
  aggregation changes.
- **`PriceBook`**, a frozen dataclass: `price_in`, `price_out`, `models` (`PRICE_MODELS`), `seconds`
  (`PRICE_SECONDS`) and `subscriptions` (`PRICE_SUBSCRIPTIONS`).
- **`parse_subscription_prices(spec)`:** comma-separated `service=usd_per_month`, keyed by the `engine` id
  (`elevenlabs=99,novelai=25`). It returns the prices and the malformed entries, as `parse_second_prices` does.
- **`analyze(records, since, until, agent=None, prices=PriceBook())`** over plain data. The days are given, and there
  is no clock inside. A line's day is `str(ts)[:10]`, its local day in `TIMEZONE`. The bounds are inclusive.
- **Atomic rows.** The lines become one `Row` per (day, agent, service, model, kind). The service is `engine`, and a
  v1 line reads as `gemini`/`api`. Each row is priced by `cost()`: one model per row, so the price is exact. Every
  total above it is a sum of these rows.
  - A subscription row is never priced; its cost is `None`, never $0 (v4.4).
  - An `api` row with no price for its model has a cost of `None`. Its model is listed under `unpriced`. A sum over
    rows counts only the priced ones and carries `complete: false` when an unpriced `api` row is in it.
- **The returned dict** (plain JSON):
  - `since`, `until`, `days`, `agent`;
  - `total`: `calls`, `failed`, `blocked`, `input`, `output`, `tokens`, `characters`, `audio_s`, `video_s`, `cost`,
    `complete`, `cost_per_day`;
  - `previous`: the same totals for the period of the same length right before, with its `since` and `until`;
  - `services`: one per engine, sorted by cost and then calls. Each has:
    - `service`, `billing` (`api`, `subscription` or `mixed`) and the totals;
    - `previous` (its cost, calls and tokens in the previous period);
    - `series` (its daily cost and calls, for a sparkline);
    - `models`, one per model with the same fields, without `series`;
  - `kinds`: one per kind, with the totals, `previous` and `share` of the period's priced cost;
  - `day_series`: one entry per day of the period, with an empty day kept:
    - `day`, `cost` (priced), `calls` and `tokens`;
    - `cost_by_service`, `calls_by_service` and `tokens_by_service` (`{service: n}`);
    - `cost_avg7`: the trailing 7-day moving average of the cost, with the days before `since` taken from the lines;
    - `cost_cumulative`: the running total;
  - `previous_cumulative`: the previous period's running total, day by day, for the comparison line;
  - `trend`:
    - `slope_per_week`: the least-squares slope of the daily cost × 7;
    - `direction`: `rising`, `falling` or `flat`. Flat is a slope under 5 % of the mean cost a day, or fewer than 3
      days with a cost;
    - `month_at_rate`: the cost a day × 30;
  - `agents`: one per agent with the totals, `previous` and its services, when `agent` is `None`, else `[]`;
  - `subscriptions`: one per `PRICE_SUBSCRIPTIONS` entry, when `agent` is `None`, else `[]`. Each has `service`,
    `monthly` and `period_cost`, the fee prorated by day as fee / the days of that day's month, summed over the
    period;
  - `unpriced`: the models with `api` calls and no price, sorted;
  - `rows`: the atomic rows for the CSV: `day`, `agent`, `service`, `model`, `kind`, `calls`, `failed`, `blocked`,
    `input`, `output`, `characters`, `audio_s`, `video_s`, `billing`, `cost`.
- **The numbers:** seconds as `round(ms / 1000, 1)`, a cost rounded to 6 places.
- **No text.** The usage lines hold none, and the function reads no other field.
- **ARCHITECTURE §Token accounting:** the analysis, the services by `engine`, and how an unpriced or subscription row
  counts.

**Dependencies:** None

**Expected result:**
`analyze()` gives every number the Analytics view and the CLI show, for any period and agent, from the lines alone.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [ ] The period is inclusive by the line's local day; a line outside it is never counted.
- [ ] The agent filter keeps that agent's lines only. `agents` and `subscriptions` appear with all agents only.
- [ ] Services group by `engine`, and models within them. The billing is kept, and a service with both kinds of
  billing is `mixed`.
- [ ] An `api` model with no price makes its cost `None`. It is listed in `unpriced`, and the sums above it are
  marked `complete: false`.
- [ ] A subscription row's cost is `None` and never $0. Its units (calls, characters, audio seconds, tokens) are
  counted.
- [ ] The previous period has the same length and ends the day before `since`.
- [ ] The prorated fee is fee × days / days-in-month, day by day (a 30-day period across two months is checked).
- [ ] `day_series` has every day of the period, empty days included. The moving average uses the 6 days before
  `since` when there are lines for them, and the running totals end at the period's cost.
- [ ] The slope, its direction (with the flat threshold) and the month at the rate are right on made-up series:
  rising, falling, flat, a single day.
- [ ] Each service, kind and agent carries its `previous`, and each service its `series`.
- [ ] No data gives zeros, empty lists and a cost of `None`, never an error.
- [ ] **Tests:** `tests/test_usage_analytics.py` with made-up lines (Gemini api, a Pro model priced by `PRICE_MODELS`,
  fal priced by `PRICE_SECONDS`, an unpriced embedding, ElevenLabs characters, Deepgram `audio_ms`, NovelAI and
  Claude subscriptions, an OpenRouter judge). The tests cover every criterion above, and `parse_subscription_prices`
  with its malformed entries.
- [ ] **Contract:** the analysis's output shape is the route's (pinned in AGORA-168); `Row.audio_ms` is added, and
  ARCHITECTURE §Token accounting is updated.
- [ ] **Gates:** lint, tests.

### AGORA-168 — `GET /api/analytics`: the period and the agent validated, `PRICE_SUBSCRIPTIONS`

**Description:**
The panel's route for the view, in `panel/app.py`, with `analytics_view` in `panel/memory.py`. It reads every
`state/*.usage.jsonl` once a request and the prices from the environment, and returns `analyze()`'s report. Compose
passes `PRICE_SUBSCRIPTIONS` to the panel.

**What needs to be done:**
- **The route:** `GET /api/analytics?since=YYYY-MM-DD&until=YYYY-MM-DD[&agent=<name>]`.
  - **Bearer**, by the existing middleware (401 without it).
  - **The agent** is resolved through the registry; an unknown name is 404, as `/api/usage` does.
  - **The dates:** both are required, ISO days, and `since` ≤ `until`. The span is at most `ANALYTICS_DAYS_MAX`
    (366) days, inclusive. Anything else is 400 with a short reason (`bad date`, `since after until`,
    `span over 366 days`). A day after today is allowed and counts nothing.
- **`analytics_view(state_dir, since, until, agent)`:**
  - an unreadable `state/` gives `{"available": false}`, never a 500;
  - otherwise `{"available": true, "priced": …, **analyze(...)}`;
  - corrupt lines are skipped, as `usage_view` does.
- **The prices:** `PRICE_INPUT_PER_1M` / `PRICE_OUTPUT_PER_1M`, `PRICE_MODELS`, `PRICE_SECONDS` and
  `PRICE_SUBSCRIPTIONS`. Each malformed entry is named in the log once, as now.
- **Compose:** `PRICE_SUBSCRIPTIONS: ${PRICE_SUBSCRIPTIONS:-}` in the panel's environment, and the line in
  `.env.example` and `server/.env.example`, empty with a comment.
- **ARCHITECTURE:**
  - §The panel: the route, its validation and the view;
  - §Configuration: `PRICE_SUBSCRIPTIONS`;
  - §Contracts: the route and its response's top-level keys.

**Dependencies:** AGORA-167

**Expected result:**
The page can ask for any period up to a year and any agent, and gets the analysis or a clear 400 / 404.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [ ] 401 without the token, and 404 for an unknown agent.
- [ ] 400 on a bad date, `since` after `until`, a span over 366 days, or a missing date.
- [ ] The response's top-level keys are pinned. An unreadable `state/` gives `{"available": false}`.
- [ ] `PRICE_SUBSCRIPTIONS` reaches the analysis, and a malformed entry is logged once and ignored.
- [ ] **Tests:** the route tests in `tests/test_panel_analytics.py`, with a temporary `state/` and made-up lines and
  the usage files read once. `tests/test_compose.py` covers the panel's `PRICE_SUBSCRIPTIONS` and both examples.
- [ ] **Contract:** the route and its response's shape, pinned by `tests/test_panel_analytics.py`; the compose
  environment, pinned by `tests/test_compose.py`; ARCHITECTURE §The panel, §Configuration and §Contracts updated.
- [ ] **Gates:** lint, tests, compose.

### AGORA-169 — The Analytics view: the period, the agent, tiles, charts and trends, services and models, kinds, agents, CSV

**Description:**
A page of its own in the panel's static app (`panel/static/app.js`, with additions to `panel.css`), opened from the
rail. It follows the design handoff's tokens and components, in an English UI, with no chart library and no outside
request.

**What needs to be done:**
- **The rail:** «Analytics» in the System group, beside Tokens. It switches the main area to the view
  (`S.view = "analytics"`). The dashboard is one click back, and the agents' drawer still opens over it.
- **The period:** presets Today, Yesterday, 7 days, 30 days, This month and Last month, or a custom From–To (two date
  inputs). Dates are local days in the panel's own today: the `until` of «today» is the server's `until`, so no
  browser clock drives it.
- **The agent:** All, or one of the registry's agents (a segmented control or a select, by count).
- **The fetch:** the view asks `/api/analytics` when the choice changes, never on the dashboard's poll. It keeps its
  data across re-renders, and a failed fetch shows a card-level error, never a blank page.
- **The address:** the choice is kept in the hash (`#analytics?range=30d&agent=kira`, or `since`/`until` for a custom
  range), so a report reopens as it was. A preset reopens as the same preset, relative to that day.
- **What it shows:**
  - **Tiles:**
    - the cost, marked «≥» when `complete` is false;
    - its change from the previous period: ± $ and %, «—» when either side is unpriced;
    - calls, failed and blocked, tokens, the cost a day;
    - the trend: an arrow, ± $ a week, and «rising», «falling» or «flat»;
    - «≈ $X a month at this rate».
  - **Charts:** inline SVG built by pure functions in the page (`svgStackedBars`, `svgLines`, `svgShare`,
    `svgSparkline`). They use the handoff's colour tokens, so both themes work, with one colour per service and a
    legend. Each mark has a native tooltip (`<title>`), and there is no chart library and no outside request.
    1. **The cost a day:** bars stacked by service, with `cost_avg7` as a line over them.
    2. **The running total:** this period's `cost_cumulative` against `previous_cumulative`, dashed, with both totals
       at the ends.
    3. **Activity a day:** the calls or tokens (a toggle) by service, as lines.
    4. **Shares:** one 100 % bar of the priced cost by service, and one of the calls by service, so the
       subscriptions show too.
    5. **Agents** (All only): the cost by agent as horizontal bars, each with its previous period's cost as a marker.
    - An empty period shows «No data for this period» in place of the charts.
  - **Services:** a table with Service, billing, a sparkline, calls, failed, tokens in/out, characters, audio s,
    video s, cost and the change.
    - The sparkline is the service's daily cost, or its calls for a subscription.
    - The change is an arrow, ± $ and %, or ± calls for a subscription.
    - A row expands to its models. Subscription rows show «subscription» instead of a cost, never $0.
    - The display names are Gemini, OpenRouter, fal.ai, ElevenLabs, Deepgram, NovelAI and Claude, keyed by engine.
  - **Kinds:** a table with kind, calls, tokens, cost, share and the change.
  - **Agents** (All only): a table with agent, calls, tokens, cost, the change and the services.
  - **Subscriptions** (All only, when set): the service, the monthly fee and the fee for the period.
  - **Unpriced models:** one line naming the `unpriced` models, when there are any.
- **Download CSV:** the period's `rows` with a header line, made in the browser from the data already fetched and
  saved as `agora-usage-<agent|all>-<since>-<until>.csv`.
  - Fields are quoted when they hold a comma, a quote or a line break, with quotes doubled.
  - A field starting with `=`, `+`, `-` or `@` gets a leading `'`, so a spreadsheet never runs it. A negative number
    never occurs.
- **No text** reaches the view: the data holds none.

**Dependencies:** AGORA-168

**Expected result:**
The owner opens Analytics, picks Кіра and 30 days or any range, and reads her spending by service, model, kind and
day, and downloads it.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [ ] The rail opens the view, and the dashboard is one click back.
- [ ] The presets and the custom range send the right `since`/`until`, and the agent choice sends `agent`.
- [ ] The hash restores the choice on reload.
- [ ] Services expand to models. Subscription rows never show $0, and a partial cost shows «≥».
- [ ] The five charts draw from the data: stacked bars with the moving average, the running totals, the activity
  lines with their toggle, the shares, and the agents with All. An empty period shows its message.
- [ ] The tiles show the change, the trend and the month at the rate. Each service and kind shows its sparkline and
  change.
- [ ] The agents and subscriptions tables appear with All only.
- [ ] The CSV has the header and the rows, quotes and the formula prefix applied.
- [ ] **Tests:** `tests/test_panel_static.py`:
  - the rail entry and the view's markers;
  - the presets' labels;
  - the chart builders present, in SVG with `<title>` tooltips, and colours from CSS variables only;
  - the call to `/api/analytics` only (no outside request, the two `fetch(` calls unchanged);
  - the CSV's header and its escaping function;
  - no secret in the page.
- [ ] **Gates:** lint, tests.
- [ ] **Manual (owner):** in the panel he opens Analytics, picks Кіра and «30 days», and sees:
  - her cost by service: Gemini, OpenRouter and fal in dollars, and ElevenLabs, Deepgram and NovelAI by their units;
  - each service's models, what it was for, and the cost a day;
  - the charts: the cost a day by service with its moving average, the running total against the previous period,
    the calls a day, the shares;
  - the trends: each service's change and sparkline, the weekly slope, and the month at this rate.

  The totals match `usage_report.py` for the same days.

  the subscriptions line, if it is set.
