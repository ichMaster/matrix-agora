## Findings

### 1. 🟡 R3's re-check precedes the voice step
`agents/agent.py` `reply` → `send_line`.

**Scenario:** the R3 check passes, then the line spends 2–4 s in TTS. Another agent's line lands, and this one follows
it.

**Before v5.3:** the gap was a single send.

**Effect:** occasionally two lines out of order. No wave limit is broken: the wave was counted at the check.

**Deferred → backlog:** re-check R3 when the voice is ready, and drop both if the room moved on. The voice credits
are already spent by then, so it is a trade-off, not a fix.

### 2. 🟡 A parameter with no caller
`agents/speech.py` `voice_message(reply_to)`.

**Deferred → cleanup:** keep or drop with the next touch of the module.

### 3. 🟡 The caption reader stays
**Kept:** about an hour of the room's history holds captioned voice-only lines. The speakers' backfill and Hermes's
digests read them as lines, and dropping it would turn them into silence in the context.

## What's solid

- **One budget, enforced before any paid call.** It covers every speaker but the heralds. A failed or over-budget
  voice is the text alone.
- **The key's reach.** `server/voice.env` is read by exactly the five members who speak; it leaves the environment
  first, before Claude's CLI can inherit it (pinned).
- **The turn-taking is unchanged.** The agents read the text messages, and the voice is an extra event no agent
  answers. Members' audio is never logged.
- **The own-questions line.** It is pure, capped (8 × 200 characters), in contract order, and never holds a banned
  word in its frame.
- **Every live change of the format** shipped with tests and green gates, and the reverted ones left no residue
  (`text_under_voice` gone).
